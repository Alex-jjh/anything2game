# Engagement flow — what makes a level pull you in (2026-11-19)

**Scope**: the flow, screens and play beats that make one level of the Case frame compelling, built on the warm "Current" look the team preferred (Case File v2 palette: contrast-fixed, cast colours kept apart from right/wrong). Storyboard of the real build on real demo content: [screens/hero-flow.png](screens/hero-flow.png) (generated by `HeroFlowScreenshotTest`, real gestures, Attention paper). Principles D1–D10 are from [design-review-2026-11-19](design-review-2026-11-19.md). Draft for the team; the team decides.

## 1. The loop in one line

**Suspicion → clues → confrontation → verdict → what comes back tomorrow.** The player is never told the answer: they collect evidence while solving small clues, then use *that same evidence* to catch the suspect's overclaim. The learning act (check a claim against the page) is the game act (D1).

## 2. The eight beats (storyboard order)

| # | Beat | What the player does | Why it pulls | Principle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Briefing** | Taps through 3 short lines; partner and suspect on stage, the speaker large, others dimmed | A stake in 10 seconds ("it claims it killed recurrence for good") and a suspect to doubt | D7 |
| 2 | **Clue: swipe** | Drags a claim card; the next card peeks behind; a SUPPORTED / NOT SUPPORTED stamp fades in with the lean; a haptic at the commit point | Physical, one-thumb, instant feedback before release | D2, D4 |
| 3 | **Verdict stamp** | Sees a CORRECT / NOT QUITE / PARTLY stamp, each with *why* and "Show evidence"; Next docked at the bottom | Informational feedback, not points; the page is one tap away | D1, D5 |
| 4 | **Doubt a claim** | Reads the suspect's numbered testimony and taps the one that goes too far | The suspect is the AI summary itself: players learn to distrust summaries | D6 |
| 5 | **Hold to object** | Holds the docked button; it fills over ~0.65 s with a haptic at the start and end; releasing early cancels | A deliberate, weighty act (no accidental objections) — the level's tension peak | D2, D3 |
| 6 | **Present evidence** | Chooses from **the evidence collected in this level** — shown as the quoted lines the clues cited, with page numbers | Clues pay off; 5 notes instead of every passage in the paper | D6 |
| 7 | **Overclaim caught** | OVERCLAIM stamp; why the claim goes beyond the evidence | The "gotcha" moment, earned by reasoning | D1 |
| 8 | **Case closed** | One big stamp lands; outcome first (score, boss, "1 to revisit tomorrow"), then the takeaways | One expressive moment per level; a reason to come back without guilt | D3, D8 |

## 3. Why someone keeps playing (the pitch version)

- **A villain who is a summary.** The suspect speaks like an AI summary that overclaims. Catching it trains the skill students actually lack: checking a confident sentence against the source.
- **Clues are ammunition.** Everything you check in the clues becomes evidence you can use against the suspect. The quiz is not bolted on; it is the investigation.
- **Your thumb does the work.** Swipe, hold, tap. Every main action sits in the thumb zone, so you can play standing on a metro one-handed.
- **Every verdict shows its page.** You can tap "Show evidence" from any stamp, which builds trust in what the AI generated.
- **Coming back is a feature, not guilt.** What you missed returns tomorrow (Leitner review), through a widget or notification. No streak shaming.
